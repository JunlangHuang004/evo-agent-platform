## 1. Demand drivers

### 1.1 Industry sales at record levels (primary source)
- Global semiconductor sales were **$120.6bn in May 2026**, up **9.2% MoM** vs April 2026 ($110.5bn) and **+104.1% YoY** vs May 2025 ($59.1bn). This was the **15th consecutive monthly record**. Monthly sales are a three-month moving average compiled by WSTS.
  - Source: Semiconductor Industry Association (SIA), "Global Semiconductor Sales Increase 9.2% Month-to-Month in May," 6 Jul 2026. https://www.semiconductors.org/global-semiconductor-sales-increase-9-2-month-to-month-in-may/ (as-of 6 Jul 2026)
- Regional YoY growth in May 2026: Americas +132.2%, Asia Pacific/All Other +118.9%, China +88.8%, Europe +60.7%, Japan +23.8%. MoM increases in all regions (China +10.7%, APAC +9.2%, Americas +8.6%, Europe +7.3%, Japan +6.4%).
  - Source: SIA, same release (as-of 6 Jul 2026)

### 1.2 AI data-center concentration is the dominant demand driver
- Up to roughly **half of industry revenues are expected to come from AI data-center chips in 2026**.
- AI data-center workloads are forecast to **triple or quadruple annually between 2026 and 2030**.
- AI network fabric spending is expected to grow at a **~38% CAGR between 2024 and 2029**.
- DRAM capex expected to rise **14%** and NAND flash capex **5%**, to **US$61bn** and **US$21bn** respectively.
  - Source: Deloitte Insights, "2026 Semiconductor Industry Outlook." https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/technology/technology-media-telecom-outlooks/semiconductor-industry-outlook.html (as-of 2026; retrieved 2026-11-22)

### 1.3 Memory/HBM and advanced packaging as bottlenecks and growth vectors
- Severe shortages in essential components such as memory are projected to drive **~50% price spikes by mid-year** (2026), redrawing the supply chain map.
- HBM is increasingly integrated closer to logic chiplets (silicon interposers or 3D stacks), enabling multi-terabyte-per-second data movement at lower joules/bit.
- Co-packaged optics (CPO) and linear pluggable optics (LPO) can reduce power consumption by **30%-50%** and improve bandwidth/TCO; adoption expected to rise in 2026.
  - Source: Deloitte Insights, "2026 Semiconductor Industry Outlook" (as-of 2026)

### 1.4 Demand-side warning signs (2027-2028 divergence risk)
Deloitte flags four reasons 2027-2028 could diverge from current expectations:
1. **ROI/monetization:** if AI monetization takes longer or is lower than expected, data-center projects could be canceled or postponed.
2. **Power:** AI data centers are expected to need **92 GW of additional electric power by 2027**; grid availability and turbine shortages are constraints.
3. **Innovation:** more efficient models/chips could reduce compute demand for the same tasks.
4. **Pricing:** new competitive chips at lower prices could be deflationary for the chip market.
  - Source: Deloitte Insights, "2026 Semiconductor Industry Outlook" (as-of 2026)

## 2. Supply and geopolitics

### 2.1 Export controls and tech sovereignty
- Governments increasingly treat AI models, chip design IP, and leading AI accelerators as critical to national security and supply-chain resilience, and are using export controls to secure local/regional availability of leading-edge AI chip manufacturing.
- Example: the **US government in December 2025 approved NVIDIA to sell H200 AI chips to a set of approved customers in China, in return for a 25% share in NVIDIA's chip sales**.
- Europe is described as caught between US export controls and China's countermeasures.
  - Source: Deloitte Insights, "2026 Semiconductor Industry Outlook" (as-of 2026)

### 2.2 China's domestic capability push
- Reports that a **Chinese state-backed manufacturer began mass-producing an immersion DUV lithography machine** — a category ASML has effectively dominated for decades — knocked ASML and equipment peers sharply lower.
- Chinese memory maker **CXMT** attracted institutional demand reportedly exceeding available shares by **more than 500x** in its Shanghai listing, reviving fears of Chinese memory expansion pressuring pricing power for Micron/SK Hynix.
  - Source: Leverage Shares, "The 2026 Semiconductor Selloff Creates an Opportunity," 24 Aug 2026. https://leverageshares.com/en-eu/insights/the-2026-semiconductor-selloff-creates-an-opportunity/ (as-of 24 Aug 2026)
  - Note: Leverage Shares is an ETP issuer publishing a marketing communication; treat as secondary/commentary, not primary evidence.

### 2.3 Regional divergence in the supply chain
- Southeast Asia and India will likely emerge as volume-based back-end assembly and test hubs; Taiwan, the US, Japan, and parts of Europe may focus on heterogeneous integration and advanced packaging.
- Talent constraints in advanced packaging may hinder US/Europe autonomy goals even as volume back-end capacity expands in Asia.
- Foreign direct investment into the rest of Asia may be affected as North America, Europe, the Middle East, and Japan ramp domestic production.
  - Source: Deloitte Insights, "2026 Semiconductor Industry Outlook" (as-of 2026)

### 2.4 Power as a supply constraint
- AI data-center buildouts may further constrain electric power grids; companies that proactively invested in power generation/availability may be advantaged.
  - Source: Deloitte Insights, "2026 Semiconductor Industry Outlook" (as-of 2026)

## 3. Valuation and market dynamics

### 3.1 The 2026 correction
- The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) surged **more than 100%** before reaching a record high in late June 2026, then fell as much as **29% from its June peak** as investors questioned whether valuations had moved ahead of earnings.
- Triggers cited: weaker-than-expected AI chip guidance from Broadcom (Q3 AI semiconductor revenue guidance of **$16bn vs ~$17.2bn expected**, with no full-year AI forecast raise); higher oil prices (Brent above **$84/bbl** on Iran/Strait of Hormuz tensions) pushing Treasury yields toward multi-decade highs; and China competition news.
- Broadcom's AI semiconductor revenue is still expected to reach **$16bn in the current quarter, growth of more than 200% YoY**.
  - Source: Leverage Shares, 24 Aug 2026 (secondary/commentary)

### 3.2 The core valuation question
- The central question: **can earnings catch up with the expectations already built into share prices?**
- "Strong industry growth does not guarantee strong stock returns" — if investors pay too much for future growth, fundamentals can take years to catch up, and some semiconductor stocks can go nowhere for an extended period.
- JPMorgan argued the pullback could create a buying opportunity; Morgan Stanley took a more cautious view on the sector in H2 2026.
  - Source: Leverage Shares, 24 Aug 2026 (secondary/commentary)

### 3.3 Deloitte's valuation-relevant framing
- If AI chip demand slows, chip designers/manufacturers benefiting from AI tailwinds could face headwinds: revenue growth could decrease or turn negative, earnings could be lower, and P/E and P/S multiples could fall.
- Because AI chips are high-value but low-volume, a revenue decline would likely have relatively little impact on fabs and toolmakers; packaging, memory, power, and communications semiconductor producers could be more affected.
  - Source: Deloitte Insights, "2026 Semiconductor Industry Outlook" (as-of 2026)
