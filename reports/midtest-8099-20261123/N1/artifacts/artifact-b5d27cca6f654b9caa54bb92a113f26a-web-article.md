# HKUST Makes AI a Required Course for Every Incoming Undergraduate

*Staff report | Published 22 November 2026 | Story subject: an HKUST announcement dated 14 July 2026 (not a November announcement)*

Starting in the 2026/27 academic year, every incoming undergraduate student at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST) will be required to complete a six-credit Artificial Intelligence (AI) Common Core Program — a university-wide mandate that makes AI literacy a graduation requirement for all new students, regardless of their major.

## What happened

HKUST announced the new requirement on 14 July 2026. Under the program, all incoming undergraduates must complete six credits of AI coursework, structured in two parts:

- **Foundations of AI Literacy: Human–AI Orchestration** — a foundational course covering the principles of working alongside AI systems.
- **AI Horizon** courses — a set of courses offered by different schools and academic departments, covering the latest AI developments and interdisciplinary applications.

The program is built around two framing concepts HKUST calls "Human–AI Co-Creation" and "Data Literacy." The university describes its goal as treating AI and intelligent agents as partners in learning, research, and innovation, and as building responsible, critical, and creative use of AI rather than passive reliance on it.

Prof. Jimmy Fung, HKUST Associate Provost (Teaching and Learning), said the program pioneers a new model of human-AI collaborative learning centered on the concept of "Human–AI Orchestration," and that AI is reshaping talent demand across industries. He added that students will learn not only how to use AI tools but also how to integrate the complementary strengths of humans and AI to drive innovation and address complex challenges. (Paraphrased from the university's announcement; the full verbatim quote is available in the source.)

The mandate builds on earlier work. HKUST introduced its "Major + X" curriculum framework in 2021/22, which allowed students to add emerging fields such as AI to their degrees. According to the university, more than 1,450 students have enrolled in AI-related courses under that initiative to date (the announcement does not define the scope or denominator of this figure).

## Why it matters

The decision signals that HKUST now treats AI literacy as a baseline competency for every graduate, not a specialization for computer science students. That is a meaningful shift for a research university: it moves AI education from an elective track into the core of the undergraduate experience.

It also reflects a broader debate in higher education about how universities should respond to generative AI — whether to restrict it, tolerate it, or teach students to use it well. HKUST's answer, at least on paper, is the third option: make structured, critical AI use part of the curriculum itself.

## What it means for students

- **A new graduation requirement.** If you are an incoming undergraduate in 2026/27 or later, you will need to plan six AI credits into your degree.
- **Courses across departments.** Because AI Horizon courses are offered by different schools and departments, students may be able to take AI coursework connected to their own field rather than only in a computer science setting.
- **A different skill set.** The emphasis on "orchestration" suggests the program is aimed at teaching students to direct and evaluate AI tools, not just to understand how they work technically.

## What it means for faculty

- **New teaching responsibilities.** Delivering AI Horizon courses across schools and departments implies new course design and teaching load distributed beyond the computing department.
- **Curriculum coordination.** The two-part structure requires alignment between a shared foundational course and department-specific offerings.
- **A teaching-and-learning agenda.** The program is framed by the Associate Provost (Teaching and Learning), indicating it sits within a broader institutional strategy on AI in education.

## Open questions

HKUST's announcement does not yet specify several details that students and faculty will need:

- Which departments will offer AI Horizon courses, and how many.
- How the six credits are divided between the foundational course and AI Horizon courses.
- How the courses will be assessed.
- Whether current (non-incoming) students are affected in any way.
- How academic integrity and data privacy will be handled in AI-based coursework.

These remain unconfirmed as of publication and are worth following up.
