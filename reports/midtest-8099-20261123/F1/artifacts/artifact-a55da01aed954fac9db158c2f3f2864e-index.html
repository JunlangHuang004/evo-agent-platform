2. The proposal: assumptions, allocation, rationale

Confirmed assumptions

Age 25; HKD 1,000,000; US-listed equities/ETFs; 10+ year horizon; moderately aggressive; long-term capital growth; USD assets acceptable; no near-term liquidity need; ETF-based; no ESG screen, no leverage, no crypto. If any of these is wrong, the allocation must be re-derived.

Rationale

  • Long horizon (10+ yrs) justifies a high equity weight; time allows recovery from drawdowns.
  • Moderately aggressive tolerance supports the 85/15 split and the growth/tech tilt, while the 15% bond+cash sleeve keeps the worst-case drawdown within the stated −30% to −40% band.
  • Diversification across US, developed ex-US and emerging markets reduces single-market concentration risk.
  • Low-cost ETFs match your stated preference and minimize fee drag over a long horizon.

Trade-offs

  • Higher equity weight = higher expected return but larger drawdowns (a severe bear market could cut the portfolio ~30–40%).
  • The growth/tech tilt raises expected return but increases concentration and volatility versus a pure total-market core.
  • International sleeves add diversification but introduce currency and geopolitical risk and have historically lagged US returns.
  • The bond sleeve reduces volatility but caps upside in strong bull markets.
  • No leverage / no crypto keeps risk bounded but forgoes higher-risk higher-return options.

5. What to do next

  1. Don't invest everything on day one. Spread the HKD 1,000,000 in over 6–12 months (dollar-cost averaging). A 50/50 split (half now, half over the next year) is a reasonable middle ground.
  2. Consider trimming the tech slice. Keep it only if you accept the extra swings, or move some into the broad US market fund.
  3. Write down a rebalancing rule. E.g. check once a year, and if any block drifts more than 5% from target, bring it back.
  4. Decide now what you'll do in a crash. Write it down: "If my portfolio falls 30–40%, I will hold and rebalance — not sell."
  5. Check the details before you trade. Confirm each fund's fees, tracking, and liquidity — we did not verify these.
  6. Re-do the plan if anything changes. New job, house purchase, or a different risk appetite means the plan should be rebuilt.
