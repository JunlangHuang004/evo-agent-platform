## 3. Why we chose this mix

- **You have time on your side.** Over 10+ years, stocks have historically recovered from bad patches. That's why we can hold a lot of stocks.
- **You said you can handle a rough year.** An 85% stock mix is only sensible if you truly won't panic-sell when it drops.
- **Spreading out reduces single-country risk.** If the US has a bad decade, your Europe, Japan and emerging-market holdings can help.
- **Low-cost funds keep more of your money working for you.** Small yearly fees add up over decades.

## 4. The risks — in plain words

Every investment has risks. Here are the ones that matter for you, ranked by how much they could hurt:

1. **The market can fall a lot (HIGH).** With 85% in stocks, a severe crash could cut your portfolio by roughly 30-40% in a bad year. That is *within* what you said you can tolerate — but it is at the edge, and it could be worse. **Treat 30-40% as an estimate, not a floor.**
2. **Too much of your money is in the same big US tech names (HIGH).** The US market and the tech fund both lean on the same giant AI/tech companies. When those fall, both parts fall together. This is the biggest structural weakness in the plan.
3. **You might sell at the worst time (HIGH).** The single most common way investors lose money is selling in a panic during a crash. This plan only works if you can hold on.
4. **Bonds won't save you in a crash (MODERATE).** The 10% bond slice is small and can also lose value when interest rates rise. It's a steadying weight, not a safety net.
5. **Inflation slowly eats buying power (MODERATE).** Over 10+ years, prices rise. Stocks are your main defence; cash is the most exposed.
6. **Currency moves (LOW-MODERATE).** Your money is in US dollars. The HKD is pegged to the USD, so this is a small risk, not a big one.
7. **Timing right now is not ideal (MODERATE-HIGH).** As of 20 November 2026 the US market was in a sharp sell-off (the S&P 500 had fallen about 3.7% in a week, tech stocks fell harder, and a fear gauge spiked). Starting all at once today means buying into a falling market.

## 5. What we suggest you do about the risks

1. **Don't invest everything on day one.** Spread your HKD 1,000,000 in over 6-12 months (a bit each month). This is called *dollar-cost averaging* — it avoids putting all your money in at a bad moment. A 50/50 split (half now, half over the next year) is a reasonable middle ground.
2. **Consider trimming the tech slice.** The 15% tech fund adds return but also adds risk. Keep it only if you accept the extra swings, or move some of it into the broad US market fund.
3. **Write down a rebalancing rule.** For example: check once a year, and if any block drifts more than 5% from its target, bring it back. This forces you to sell high and buy low automatically.
4. **Decide now what you'll do in a crash.** Write it down: *"If my portfolio falls 30-40%, I will hold and rebalance — not sell."* Deciding in advance is far easier than deciding in the middle of a panic.
5. **Check the details before you trade.** Confirm each fund's fees, how closely it tracks its index, and how easily it trades. We did not verify these fund-level details.
6. **Re-do the plan if anything changes.** New job, house purchase, different risk appetite — any of these means the plan should be rebuilt.

## 7. Glossary — the jargon, translated

- **ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund):** a basket of many investments that trades like a single stock. Buying one share gives you a slice of everything inside.
- **Equity / stock:** part-ownership of a company.
- **Bond:** a loan you make to a government or company; they pay you interest and return your money at the end.
- **Fixed income:** the umbrella term for bonds and similar lending investments.
- **Allocation:** how your money is divided among different investments.
- **Diversification:** spreading money around so one bad bet doesn't ruin you.
- **Drawdown:** the drop from the highest point your portfolio reached to a lower point.
- **Volatility:** how much the value bounces around; high volatility means bigger swings.
- **Rebalancing:** periodically resetting your mix back to its target percentages.
- **Dollar-cost averaging (DCA):** investing a fixed amount at regular intervals instead of all at once.
- **Expense ratio:** the yearly fee a fund charges, shown as a percentage of your money.
- **Leverage:** borrowing money to invest more than you have — riskier; you said no.
- **FX / currency risk:** the risk that exchange rates move against you.
- **VIX:** a widely used "fear gauge" that rises when investors are nervous.
- **P/E ratio:** price divided by earnings — a rough measure of whether a stock is expensive.
- **Emerging markets:** developing economies (e.g. China, India, Brazil) with higher growth and higher risk.
